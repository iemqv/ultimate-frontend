# Compose a place, not a landing page

## For a short open brief, build the destination first

The visitor is already here. Don't make a page advertising a second part of the same page. Decide what the visitor came to encounter or accomplish, then open directly **inside that experience**. Start by designing its actual resting state and its most revealing next state. An image with a button that only enlarges it isn't yet a subject-specific interaction. If stillness is the right medium, make the relationship among the actual pieces do the work. Compare two possibilities from different properties of the subject using [Direction studies](direction-studies.md); don't simply choose a style name.

Build those states as the entry page. Give the subject generous scale; fit the title, material and actions to the same composition. Make navigation change what the person can see, choose or do. Type can be expressive without a subordinate marketing paragraph. Study the settled desktop and phone frames before extending the work. [Build the frame](build-the-frame.md) covers media, crop and working edges. Keep a good typographic or interactive signature; don't manufacture a caption to justify it.

**Default architecture for an unspecified creative brief: one destination, multiple meaningful states.** Expansion happens *within* that destination. New content alters the material, sequence, scale or decision; it does not automatically add a section. Do not append a separate featured-work grid, process explainer, about panel or contact pitch to prove you built a complete site. If the brief explicitly calls for multiple sections or pages, build them. Rich editorial material can warrant a continuous scroll; compose it as one sustained story, not self-contained modules. More than one area is justified by a visitor need, not by website convention. Never fake a business transaction to provide an ending.

## Place actual material; never start with tiles

For an open creative brief the rule is literal: **no three peer visual blocks side by side in the default view, whether cards, borderless figures, works, techniques or services**. Four instead of three, staggered cards, or a deck turned into a carousel are not remedies. A filter the agent invented does not make this a user-requested catalog. An expressly requested comparison table, supplied grid or stock-management interface belongs to a different route. Do not destroy useful regularity there.

Work out where attention moves between the first state and the next and which details deserve space. Some ideas need generous silence, some a tight run of related material. No house palette, punctuation mark, pair of fonts, preferred gallery widget or effects stack. A structural idea is one that dies when you replace the subject, not just its artwork.

## Write the visible strings *before* the HTML

In working notes, list the exact title, proper names, actions, input labels and decision-changing facts. A creative page with no supplied prose starts with that vocabulary and nothing else. No intro paragraph, category eyebrow, count subtitle, specimen ordinal, explanatory card line or closing motto. Keep functional directions, units, prerequisites, error messages, authored writing and meaningful accessible names. Add an unsupplied sentence only if you can identify what someone would otherwise get wrong at that moment. Don't narrate a visible count, color, setting or state. Don't repeat a category from the title or a work name five centimeters away. Leave space unfilled.

## Acceptance: reject the rendered result, not just the outline

- Try entering directly, choosing or manipulating the material and reaching a useful result on desktop and phone, without reading a pitch first. Test keyboard activation, visible state, exit/recovery and reduced motion with [Interaction](interaction.md).
- Inspect the first view, a different state, and the middle if there is a scroll. Look at the outlines without reading the words. **Three similar picture/label blocks in one horizontal band? Stop.** Replace that whole band with a way to encounter its material *in the primary experience*. Hiding the third at a breakpoint or stripping its border is not a fix.
- Read the actual visible strings in DOM order. Compare them with the list. Remove nodes that only announce or narrate the experience, then adjust the space. Preserve required information, labels and accessibility. Inspect [Unslop signs](signs.md) in context, then do the [finish check](finish-check.md). Without a browser, say which visual and interaction claims remain unverified.
