# Compose the change, not just the resting screen

Use this method when one decision affects several elements. A scene is the part of a task someone can hold in mind: a reader follows a footnote and resumes, a dispatcher assigns a job and checks the new owner, or a shopper checks an option against a constraint. A page can contain several scenes. Don't turn each one into a panel.

## Find the hinge

Use one real item and one difficult case. Imagine the visitor just before and after the consequential choice. What did they need to keep in view? Which thing needs more attention now, and which can recede? Try describing both states without naming a UI component. If the answer is “see more information,” identify the actual information and how it changes the decision. If the material only needs to be read, the hinge might be the next paragraph or figure, not a click.

## Give the elements parts to play

In the resting state identify an anchor (the thing being examined or changed), a cue (the evidence needed to interpret it) and a way onward. They need not be three separate elements. Work with the actual material at the scale it will occupy. Group what must be read together by proximity and a common edge; distinguish what must be compared by a stable axis, label or sequence. Reserve the strongest contrast for the thing that warrants attention *now*. A title, graphic and button cannot all be the lead. Don't sacrifice legibility to force a dramatic scale contrast.

Build the next state on the same real item. Carry an identifier or spatial reference through the transition; place the effect where the eye or hand will look for it. Decide whether the action should change the anchor itself, reveal something next to it or move into a separate view. Base that choice on what needs to stay visible. Use the least mechanism that preserves the necessary context. A page jump might serve a long article; it can be costly for a rapid sequence of decisions. Motion can explain a relationship but must not be the only evidence that anything changed.

## Recompose under pressure

At phone width, read the scene at its actual size. Can someone see the evidence at the moment they need it? Stacking every module may leave the control a screen away from the result. Shrinking everything may preserve alignment while making the content useless. Change grouping, density or controls before losing the defining relationship. Preserve a keyboard path and a predictable return. Check an awkward label, a second selection or a reversed action: a single beautiful default can conceal a broken next state.

## Look for the broken link

Inspect two renders, before and after the action, and actually perform it. Point at the visible change and the part that stayed stable. If you can explain the concept only by referring to your design notes, change the screen. If the evidence is legible, the changed state is clear and nothing competes without a purpose, stop. No score, compulsory animation or additional mockup is required.
