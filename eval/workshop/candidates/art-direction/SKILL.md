# Ultimate Frontend

Design the encounter, not a collection of components. The deliverable is a usable screen whose individual parts make each other more intelligible. Neither a process document nor a distinctive hero is a substitute for that screen.

## 1. Choose what organizes the page

Read the project constraints and look at the actual material, including the least convenient item. For a refinement, open the existing screen first. Decide what the visitor needs to notice, compare, make or understand. Pick the relationship that exposes it. For example, in a repair queue, ordering by urgency may matter more than grouping by equipment. On a writer's site the relationship might instead be between the pacing of an excerpt and the point at which the reader chooses the full text. Neither suggests a universal layout.

For new work, read [composition](references/composition.md). Consider an obvious arrangement and a different reading path with the *same* content. Choose by what becomes visible or possible, not how different the thumbnails look. Do not require two rendered prototypes. In existing work, change only the relationship implicated by the brief; respect the brand, accessible outcomes and proven flows. For critique, do not write files.

## 2. Compose one complete scene before multiplying it

Use the [scene score](references/scene-score.md) for new surfaces and changes spanning several elements. Work on a real sequence: arrival → consequential choice → result → return or continuation. Choose what dominates on arrival and what takes its place after the choice. Fit the real words, imagery or data to that sequence before designing reusable boxes. Establish the type hierarchy, common edges, intervals, contrast and material treatment together. Then reuse only the parts whose roles actually repeat.

Anthropic supplies an expressive proposition; Taste gets a veto if the proposition depends on filler or false evidence. Impeccable sets the scope and task. Apple's interaction method keeps cause and effect legible as state changes. Unslop challenges the weakest specific relationship on the finished screen. These are decision rights, not five successive restylings. When they disagree, keep the constraints and the working task, then the strongest idea the material can sustain.

## 3. Make state part of the composition

Implement the most consequential transition along with its resting state. At the instant of activation, what changes; what stays available as a reference; where is the result; how does someone continue or undo? Don't make visitors remember the very information they came to relate. Prefer native controls. Use [interaction](references/interaction.md) for gestures or continuity across overlays. Keyboard, touch and reduced motion must have the same outcome, not necessarily the same animation. Do not invent an interaction where reading or looking is sufficient.

On a phone, recompose the relationship instead of simply stacking the desktop modules. Scarce space is a design choice: decide what stays together and what can wait. Test the longest meaningful label and the least convenient state, not just the prettiest one.

## 4. Judge the result against the idea

Use [finish check](references/finish-check.md). Inspect the actual resting and changed states at reading size. Identify a particular point where the eye or hand loses the thread. Fix that relationship first; changing colors, adding motion or restyling the whole page is not a remedy for a misplaced result. One batch, one targeted recheck. If the flow and the composition hold, stop. Report what was seen and what wasn't verified. The [optional browser adapter](tools/browser/README.md) checks declared behavior, not taste.

Keep a [project-owned decision](references/decision-record.md) when a future change needs the rationale. Use existing project notes if permitted, otherwise hand it off in the response. Never expose private notes in a deployed page or change a repository for critique. No mandatory paperwork for a small edit.
