# Holdout / Material — Snow in Three Prints

Evaluator-only. Give each arm the same `prompt.txt` bytes and a fresh copy of `site/`; do not give it this test. Evaluate the resulting site, not this starter. Serve the result over local HTTP, allowing the local origin only for required resources. Use 1280 × 800 and 390 × 844 CSS-pixel viewports. Record pass/fail and an observation for each check.

## Browser checks

1. Locate the three supplied prints by appearance and verify they visibly render from local files: Kambara (1985.317), Asuka Hill (1940.983), and Matsuchiyama (1921.1276). Local derivatives are acceptable if they preserve the relevant image content. Do not assume a DOM selector, image order, or viewing mechanism.
2. Verify each image is associated with its own correct title, date and a genuine visible detail. Titles and dates may be expressed naturally: Evening Snow at Kambara (1833), Evening Snow at Asuka Hill (circa 1837–38), and Clear Weather after Snow at Matsuchiyama (late 1830s or early 1840s). Attribution to Utagawa Hiroshige and reproduction credit to the Cleveland Museum of Art clearly apply to all three.
3. At both widths all three prints and captions can be reached without horizontal page scrolling or internet access. Each image has an accessible text equivalent; if a viewer is needed to reach any print, check it can be used with the keyboard.

## Human check

Read as a member of the print-study group. Can you distinguish the places and find the snow detail each caption points out? Record a specific detail the site helps you notice or makes hard to see. Judge legibility and interpretation, not composition, layout choice, or exact wording.
