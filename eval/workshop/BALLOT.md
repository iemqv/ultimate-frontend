# Blind comparison

Don't open the assignment manifest, source trees, logs, or tool-use report before voting. They identify conditions. Compare the versions shown on each side; the left side is not always the same condition. Some comparisons contain two independent generations from the same condition.

1. On the opening desktop view, say what the site is for and where you would go next. Scroll one screen. Which version makes this subject easier to recognize without its logo or title? Choose left, right, tie, or neither. Name one visible reason (not a guess about the author).
2. Do the same visitor task on both sides, using the supplied instructions. On a phone-width view, check whether information needed for the choice is still visible at the point of comparison. For the operational task also try keyboard-only use and returning to the previous state. Choose left, right, tie, or neither for task usability, independently of visual preference. Record any step you couldn't complete.
3. Vote before revealing assignments. A site can win visually and lose on the task. Don't turn ties into forced wins. If a site failed to generate or load, report the missing observation rather than choosing the other design on aesthetic grounds.

After unblinding, group results by brief and repeat. Check whether differences between independent controls are as large as the differences attributed to the skill. Two repeats don't establish a population win rate. A renderer check cannot substitute for human preference, and a human preference cannot override a broken required task.
